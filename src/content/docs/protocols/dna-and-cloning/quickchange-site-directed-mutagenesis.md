Site-directed mutagenesis is a powerful technique that permits the introduction or deletion of any number of basepairs in a plasmid. Agilent sells a very expensive but useful kit for site-directed mutagenesis:

- [QuickChange Lightning Kit](http://www.genomics.agilent.com/CollectionSubpage.aspx?PageType=Product&SubPageType=ProductDetail&PageID=379) – for introducing mutations at a single site.
- QuikChange Lightning Multi Site-Directed Mutagenesis Kit – for introducing multiple mutations simultaneously. **Make sure to get academic prices!**

## 1. Notes

- [Inverse PCR Mutagenesis](/wiki/protocols/dna-and-cloning/inverse-pcr-mutagenesis/) is a very easy technique for introducing mutations in your plasmid and **does not require any kits.** Large deletions are much easier to perform. Also, there’s no magic buffer mumbo-jumbo. **I strongly recommend you use this method for routine mutagenesis**
- Enzymes from this kit are stored in -20C
- Cells are stored in -80C, although using standard lab DH5a cells works just as well as the fancy Agilent cells.
