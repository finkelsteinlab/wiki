Agarose gel electrophoresis is used to separate DNA, or RNA molecules by size, however proteins can also be separated on agarose gels. Separation is achieved by moving negatively charged nucleic acid molecules through an agarose matrix in an electric field. Smaller/shorter molecules move faster and migrate further than longer ones.

## 3. Pouring the Agarose

Almost all of our gels will use routine agarose from Genesee Scientific or Fisher. Solid agarose is added into [1x TAE buffer](/wiki/recipes/electrophoresis-buffers/) to a desired final concentration (weight/volume).

    Example: to make 250 mL of 1% agarose, add 2.5 grams of agarose into 250 mL of 1x TAE buffer. 

1. Heat the TAE-agarose mixture in a microwave, with gentle swirling every minute or so until the agarose is **completely** dissolved. The mixture will need to be very close to boiling to make sure the agarose is dissolved completely.

:::danger

Make sure that the very hot solution does not bump. Point bottle away from your face and body at all times.

:::

2. Cool the desired amount of agarose to ~55 °C by either leaving it on the bench or putting it in a water bath. Agarose will start to solidify below ~50 °C.
3. Add Ethidium Bromide to a final concentration of 0.5 μg/ml. Mix the gel solution thoroughly by gentle swirling. **Ethidium Bromide** is toxic and gloves should be worn at all times.
4. Cast the gel in our Bio-Rad [casting apparatus](/wiki/instruments/gel-electrophoresis-systems/).

## 4. Running the Gel

1. Place the gel in the running tank and fill the tank with 1x TAE to the fill line.
2. Mix your DNA samples with [6x running dye](/wiki/recipes/gel-loading-dyes/) and load the gel. Make sure to include the appropriate DNA ladder.
3. Run the gels at constant voltage. Running the gels too fast will cause heating and melting of the agarose. This will lead to smeary gels.
   - Small Bio-Rad gels: ~110V max recommended
   - Large Bio-Rad gels: ~90V max recommended

## 5. Visualizing the Gel

:::danger

Dispose of Ethidium Bromide contaminated waste appropriately.

:::

1. Place the gel in a tupperware dish with DI water and rock gently for 10 minutes.
2. Repeat the gel washing 1-2 more times.
3. Place on the UV transilluminator and take a picture of the gel.
4. Dispose of the gel in Ethidium Bromide waste.
