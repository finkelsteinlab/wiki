## 1. Materials

- **Zymolyase**: Chris Yellman in the Marcotte lab said: *The enzyme is specified by activity level, and that is a 100T activity enzyme.  You may find zymo from different suppliers and I think it will work just as well, but pay attention to the activity level number so that you know how much to use.  I think the enzyme is also frequently sold with activity level 20T.* In the past we used zymolyase from Seikagaku but they discontinued its production.
- **Yeast lysis solution**: 1X TE, 2% SDS, 1M NaCl. You will need to microwave for 10-30 seconds until dissolved.
- **SCE**
- **PCR cleanup kit**

## 2. Protocol

1. **Grow and harvest the cells**
   1. Grow cells overnight in liquid culture to stationary phase (OD<sub>595</sub> of 1.5 is probably preferable, but 2.5 worked once).
   2. Add 1ml cell culture to a microcentrifuge tube. Centrifuge for 1 min at 5000 RPM.
   3. Remove the supernatant from each tube, add 1ml cell culture again, and repeat the centrifugation.
   4. Discard supernatant from each tube.
2. **Digest the cell walls with zymolyase**
   1. Resuspend each sample in 96µl SCE and 4µl of 10mg/ml (100T activity) zymolyase. Vortex to mix.
   2. Incubate at 37°C with occasional gentle shaking.
   3. Digestion should occur after 30-90 minutes. Cells should be dark grey under a phase contrast microscope.
3. **Lyse the spheroplasts and precipitate protein**
   1. For each sample, prepare 100µl yeast lysis solution and add to each tube. Vortex for a few seconds.
4. **Purify the DNA using a standard PCR cleanup kit**
   1. Add 500µl of PB solution (from PCR cleanup kit) to each sample.
   2. Spin at maximum speed in microcentrifuge for 5 minutes to pellet the cell debris and precipitated protein.
   3. Apply the clear supernatant to a DNA minicolumn and proceed with purification according to the kit’s instructions.
5. **Store DNA**
   1. Leave the DNA in the elution buffer and store at -20°C.
6. **Diluting the DNA**
   1. The resulting product can be diluted at least 100x and still be viable for PCR. Further dilution is probably possible.
