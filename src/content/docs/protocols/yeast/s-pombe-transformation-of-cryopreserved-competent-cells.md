## 1. Reagents & media

- 30% glycerol in 0.1M LiAc pH4.9
- 50% PEG4000
- minimal medium with low carbon source (I have been using appropriately supplemented SD with 3% glucose)
- 1/2 YE (0.25% yeast extract, 1.5% glucose)

## 2. Cryopreservation of competent cells

1. Grow cells to 10^7 cells/ml in minimal medium with low carbon source at 30 °C (paper uses SLD 0.67% Bacto yeast nitrogen base, 0.5% glucose).
2. Place cells on ice for 15 minutes before harvesting.
3. Pellet cells at 1600 x g for 5 minutes and wash 3 times in ice cold sterilized water.
4. Resuspend cells in ice cold 30% glycerol in 0.1M LiAc pH 4.9 to 10^9 cells/ml.
5. Make 50ul aliquots and incubate for 30 minutes on ice to allow glycerol to permeate cells then place directly into -80°C freezer.

## 3. Transformation of cryopreserved cells

1. Thaw cells at 30°C in a water bath for 2 minutes.
2. Mix with 5-10ng of DNA (sufficient for plasmid transformation but worth adding 1-2ug for integration) and 145ul of 50% PEG4000.
3. Vortex gently and incubate at 30°C for 1 hour.
4. Heat shock at 43°C for 15 minutes, allow to cool to room temperature for 10 minutes.
5. Pellet cells at 1600 x g for 3 minutes and remove supernatant by pipetting.
6. Resuspend in 500ul ½ YE medium.
7. Incubate at 30°C for 1 hour with shaking.
8. Spread directly onto minimal selection plates and incubate at 30°C.

## 4. Notes

The paper states that steps 5, 6 & 7 can be omitted, plating straight after the 43°C heat shock and cooling steps does not affect transformation efficiency

For plasmid transformation expect several hundred colonies with this protocol. For chromosomal integration I have had varying results, from below 10 to nearly 200 colonies per plate.
