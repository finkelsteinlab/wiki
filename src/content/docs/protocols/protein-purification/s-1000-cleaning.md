If you are observing degraded DNA in your single molecule experiments, and have traced the problem to the gel filtration step, then cleaning the S-1000 is a good idea. This cleaning has been used successfully multiple times.

## 1. Peristaltic Pump

To avoid introducing DNAse and Protinase into either FPLC, and to keep the FPLCs free during this lengthy process , use the Amersham Pharmacia parasitical pump

- At the 1x setting and set to 5, the rate has been measured to be 0.46+/-.01 ml/min (Avg. +/- S.D.).
- DO NOT assume the settings behave is linearly. If you want a different flow rate, TEST a different setting. You are a scientist, right?

### 1.1 Before attaching your column

1. Use 100% methanol and syringes to remove all the bubbles from the Peristaltic Pump.
2. Clean the pump with 0.1M NaOH, 0.1M HCl, and H20 (injecting 10ml with syringes is fine).
3. Make sure to have the proper fittings on the pump lines to attach your columns.
4. Connect your column without bubbles, The best way to do this is to use a column storage/shipping device.

## 2. Protocol

1. (Day 1) Make 2L of 40mM Tris-Cl (7.5) + 150mM NaCl buffer.
2. Take 1L of buffer and add DNAse to a final concentration of 100 units/ml. (5mL of 20,000 units/mL stock into 1L of buffer).
3. Flow at least 2CV into the column overnight.
4. (Day 2) In the remaining 1L of buffer, add 2units/mL of protinase K final. (2.5mL of 800 units/mL stock into 1L of buffer).
5. Flow at least 2CV into the column overnight.
6. (Day 3) After flowing in 2CV Protinase K, turn off the pump and incubate an additional night.
7. (day 4) Make 2L of 40mM Tris-Cl (7.5) + 1M NaCl buffer.
8. Flowing in at least 5CV over approximately 24 hours to remove the remaining DNAse and Protinase.
9. (Day 5) attach the column to the FPLC, equilibrate into Te+ 150mM NaCl, and the colomn is ready to purify Lambda DNA again!
