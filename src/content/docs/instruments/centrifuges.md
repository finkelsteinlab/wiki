## 1. Optima XE

<img src="/wiki/media/instruments/optima_xe.jpg" width="250" alt="Optima" /> The Optima XE is the ultracentrifuge. The max speed is 90000 rpm!

- You can find the instructional video [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UNrg_TOpFhc)
  - This is intense, please sit down. Make yourself a latte.

### 1.1 Rotors

The rotor for the Optima XE is Ti-45 (titanium 45000 rpm).

- The rotors will be stored in the cold room (4C) to keep our experiments happy
  - Store the rotor in it’s blue bag for longevity.
- To clean the rotor, remove the o-ring on the lid and clean out gunk monthly. The lid is not titanium and will corrode if not cleaned. Replace the o-ring and add a little vacuum grease.
- If you clean the rotor with water or alcohol turn it upside down to dry.

### 1.2 General operation

1. When you enter the lab in the morning the Optima will be turned off and the lid will be closed.
2. Turn the Optima on.
3. Initiate vacuum seal to bring temperature to operating level.
4. If running a sample:
   1. Place samples in centrifuge tubes that can withstand appropriate force.
   2. Balance tubes so the weight is distributed equally on the rotor.
   3. Place rotor onto the motor and screw on lid.
   4. Close the lid of the Optima and set your parameters i.e. Speed, Temperature, Time.
   5. Press Start.
      - You will hear the vacuum pump turn on.
      - As the rotor approaches the speed you want, the pressure in the centrifuge drops holding the rotor in place
5. At the end of the day, when no one is running a sample raise the temperature to 28°C and initiate the vacuum.
6. After 30 minutes shut the machine off. This is to ensure no condensation develops in the machine and for the duration of the vacuum pumps.

### 1.3 Common problems

- Use bottles with the proper g-force.
- If there are fracture lines in the bottles, they need to be replaced.
- If a sample breaks in the centrifuge, the sample may leak into the vacuum pump
  - The vacuum will not seal properly if this happens.
  - Call maintenance.

### 1.4 Maintenance

- Manual
- **Cleaning**:
  - *Daily*
    - Wipe the inside of machine down.
    - Clean bottles
  - *Monthly*
    - Remove o-ring on rotor lid and clean gunk from the groove. Re-grease o-ring and replace.
  - *Annually or if worn looking*
    - Replace barcode reader
      - See Manual

## 2. Avanti J-26XP

<img src="/wiki/media/instruments/avantij26s_sm178.jpg" width="250" alt="Avanti" /> The Avanti has a max speed of 26000 rpm

- The video for this is less [ultra](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dNuiC78DJvU)

### 2.1 Rotors

The rotors for the Avanti J-26 are the JLA 16.250 and the JLA 8.1

- These rotors will be stored in the cold room.
  - Store them in their blue bags.
- To clean the rotor, remove the o-ring on the lid and clean out gunk monthly. The lid is not titanium and will corrode if not cleaned. Replace the o-ring and add a little vacuum grease.
  - If you clean the rotor with water or alcohol turn it upside down to dry.
- **DO NOT** immerse the JLA 8.1 in water. IT WILL NEVER DRY!!
  - Please wipe it down with a damp cloth or something.

### 2.2 General operation

1. When you enter the lab in the morning the Avanti will be turned off and the lid will be open.
2. Turn on the Avanti and close the lid.
3. Set temperature to 4°C and allow time to equilibrate.
4. If running a sample:
   1. Place sample in centrifuge bottles and distribute them evenly in the rotor.
      - *Pro-tip:* If your samples weigh approximately the same when inside their centrifuge adapter, you can balance them without adding water to your sample by putting some water into an empty slot in the adapter itself.
   2. Place loaded rotor onto motor and close lid. Make sure to align the pins on rotor to set in between the baffles on the motor.
   3. Set parameters i.e. Time, Temperature, Speed.
   4. Press start.
5. At the end of the day when no one is running a sample, leave the lid open and turn off the machine
   - This will ensure that the condensate evaporates.

### 2.3 Common problems

- The canisters on the JLA 8.1 can be removed from the rotor. Every few years the stress relievers (fancy o-rings) will need to be replaced.
- The canisters themselves will need to be replaced on June 1, 2020. They have the expiration date written on them.
- Use bottles that can withstand the amount of g’s you are applying.
- The rotors on the Avanti have pins that sit between two baffles on the motor.
  - Make sure these pins are between the baffles or else the rotor won’t spin with the motor.
  - These pins may break off ($500 ea!) and the baffles may wear down.
  - Please align the pins. They are $500 each.

## 3. Bench top centrifuges

<img src="/wiki/media/instruments/benchtop_centrifuge.jpg" width="250" alt="Centrifuge 5424" /> We have two benchtop centrifuges in the lab. One in Instrument Row and one in the FPLC refrigerator at 4°C.

### 3.1 General protocol

1. Set parameters: temperature, time, and speed
2. Balance samples in Eppendorf tubes
3. Place Eppendorf tubes on opposites sides of the rotor to balance
4. Place lid on rotor (optional)
5. Spin real fast
